# Story 3 — The Long Way Home (working excerpt)

Tam took the long way home, which everyone in Hollowbrook understood to mean the
way past the Edge, and no one said so out loud.

Past the last crossing-stone the trees changed their minds about being friendly.
He walked until the path forgot it was a path, and then he found it: a clearing
deep in the Edge where small magic runs large — where the little courtesies of
the village, the warmed doorknobs and the bread that never staled, stood up tall
as trees and did not care to be polite.

He did not stay long. On the way back the brook kept pace with him, quiet as a
held breath, and he thought of the old joke that wasn't a joke: the ferryman
knows the other bank. Nobody in Hollowbrook had ever needed a ferry. Tam
wondered, for the first time, what the other bank was the other bank of.

He was late for the proving. Maren said nothing, which was worse than anything
she could have said.
