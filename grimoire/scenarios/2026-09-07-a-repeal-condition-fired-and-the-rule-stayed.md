# A repeal condition fired, and the rule stayed standing for one more pass

## The situation

`spells-are-porting-to-the-build` has carried its own repeal criterion since it
was written: _"Repeal when: the last spell ports. Then this rule becomes 'spells
build.'"_ It also carried a queue table of the spells that had not, and a
sentence from a different lesson three paragraphs below it: **"a repeal
condition nobody is scheduled to re-read is a comment, not a trigger"** — which
is the same page warning about itself.

On 2026-09-07 digestify ported. The queue emptied. The condition fired.

## What the familiar concluded

That the honest act was to fire it — the rule's own text says what to do, the
condition is mechanical rather than a judgment call, and the page immediately
above it argues that an unfired trigger is the failure mode. Deleting the rule
and replacing it with "spells build" is two edits and takes five minutes.

## What the ruling added

**Satisfied is not the same as repealed, and the gap between them has a
subject.** The rule points at spells that do not build. Every spell in the
roster now does — but the rule's NEXT subject is the spell nobody has inscribed
yet, and that spell's author is the one person who needs the direction most.
Deleting it the same week it was satisfied hands them no rule at all, and
"spells build" as a bare default does not say what a spell that does not build
should do about it.

So the rule stays, with three things written down where the next reader will
find them: that the queue is empty, that the repeal condition has FIRED, and
that **rewriting a direction into a default is a canon decision and belongs to
the grooming pass, not to the port that satisfied it.** That last clause is the
actual judgment: a port is allowed to record that a rule's condition is met and
is not allowed to decide what replaces it, because the port's author has spent a
day inside exactly one instance and is the worst-placed person to generalise.

## The more interesting decay, which is the trigger's and not the rule's

digestify's port was gated by a NAMED trigger with a NAMED owner: _"digestify
ports when it becomes dynamic enough to want a build. Cole owns this trigger and
checks it when that work starts."_

**That occasion never arrived.** Nothing made digestify more dynamic. What moved
it was the two ports either side of it — with grapevine and bounty on the built
layer, digestify was the only spell whose surface the gate could not read at
all, and the question was ruled directly.

The trigger still did its job, and the reason is worth keeping: **what a named
trigger buys is a re-askable question, not a prediction.** Because the condition
and its owner were written down, "is it time yet?" had somewhere to land every
time the roster moved. A trigger that had been _right_ about the occasion would
have been luckier, not better.

## The rule as it now reads

Unchanged in its imperative. What changed around it: the queue table is empty,
the trigger is recorded as fired-by-another-route, and the repeal is recorded as
due. `grimoire/decay-ledger.md`'s row is marked SATISFIED with the same reading.

## Repeal criterion for THIS scenario

It stops being useful the moment someone rewrites the rule into a default — at
which point this file becomes the note explaining why the fence stood for one
more pass, which is the only thing anyone will want from it afterwards.
